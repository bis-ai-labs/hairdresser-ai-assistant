# Example Booking and Cancellation Policy

> Fictional course material for a local portfolio demonstration. This is an example for discussion, not a universal rule or legal advice. A real business should choose terms that fit its customers, platform, and applicable requirements.

## Before adopting a policy

Write booking terms in plain language and show them before a client confirms an appointment. Make sure the terms match the booking system and the way the business actually operates. Consider getting qualified advice about local consumer, payment, and accessibility requirements before charging deposits or cancellation fees.

## Fictional example

The imaginary studio in this lesson uses the following example terms:

- Clients can request a change or cancellation at least 24 hours before the appointment.
- Changes requested with less notice are handled by contacting the studio; the studio reviews each situation rather than promising an automatic outcome.
- A booking deposit, if used, is disclosed before checkout along with when it is applied or returned.
- If the stylist needs to reschedule, the client is contacted and offered a new time or the applicable refund option described in the booking terms.
- The appointment confirmation includes the service, expected duration, price estimate, location, and a way to ask questions.

These terms are invented teaching examples, not recommendations that every stylist should adopt. The example deliberately leaves case-by-case decisions for the studio to communicate clearly.

## Make the process easy to follow

1. Put the policy near the booking action, not only in a hard-to-find document.
2. Use specific, understandable timing and explain any deposit or fee before payment.
3. Send a confirmation with the appointment details and the policy link.
4. Provide a clear contact route for changes, accessibility needs, and exceptional circumstances.
5. Review the policy periodically using customer feedback and the business's actual scheduling experience.
