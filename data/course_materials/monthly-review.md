# A Monthly Business Check-In

> Fictional course material for a local portfolio demonstration. The checklist is educational and is not accounting, tax, or financial advice.

## Start with a clear period

Choose a month and use consistent dates. Record what each number represents and whether it is based on appointments booked, services completed, invoices issued, or money received. These are different measures.

## Review a few useful measures

- **Bookings requested:** how many appointment requests came in?
- **Appointments completed:** how many services were actually provided?
- **Cancellations and no-shows:** how many appointments changed or were missed, and what patterns are visible?
- **Collected service revenue:** how much money was received for completed services during the chosen period?
- **Business expenses:** what costs were paid or recorded, and are any transactions still uncategorized?
- **Cash movement:** compare money received and money paid during the period, using consistent records.
- **Capacity:** how many appointment hours were available and how many were used?

## Turn observations into a small next step

Write down one thing that went well, one question to investigate, and one action to try next month. For example, if several clients requested a particular time slot, the stylist could review the schedule before changing availability.

Do not infer a cause from one month's numbers alone. Check whether the records cover the same period and use the same definitions. Revenue, cash flow, taxable income, and profit are not interchangeable. Ask a qualified professional for accounting or tax guidance.
